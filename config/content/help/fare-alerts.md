## What it does

The form at the foot of every page takes an email address and keeps it. That
is the whole of what happens: the address goes on a list, and the list is real
and stored.

## What it does not do

Nothing reaches you. There is no mailer in this project and nothing in it that
could send one, so no fare is watched on your behalf and no note goes out when
a price moves.

The form used to say otherwise, in three places at once: a heading about your
inbox, a line about being told when a fare dropped, and a consent line saying
the address was kept in order to do it. All three were promises the site had no
way to keep, and they were removed rather than made true.

## Then why is it here

Because keeping the address is the honest half of the feature, and the half
that can be built without pretending. If a mailer ever arrives, the list is
already there and already has people on it.

It is also worth knowing what a form like this does with what you type, which
is the point of saying so plainly rather than leaving you to assume the usual.

## Joining twice

The list holds each address once. Give one that is already on it and you are
told so, rather than being quietly added again — the same answer, whichever it
was, so nothing about the reply reveals who else is on the list.

## Getting off it

There is nothing to unsubscribe from, since nothing is sent. What is stored is
the address itself, and [Privacy](/privacy) covers what this site keeps and why.
