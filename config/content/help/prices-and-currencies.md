## Pick a currency, anywhere

The currency control sits in the header on every page. Open it, search the list
or scroll it, and the whole site reprices — results, the calendar, checkout and
the bookings you already have.

Your choice is remembered in this browser for a year. There are no accounts
here, so a different browser or device starts again on Canadian dollars.

## What the rate is, and when it is from

Fares are held in Canadian dollars and converted for display. The rates come
from the European Central Bank, and the panel names the day they were
published rather than implying they are live — a confidently wrong date is the
one thing a converted price must not carry. If no rates have been fetched, it
says it is using built-in reference figures instead of naming a date.

Because everything converts from one held figure, the parts always add up: the
fare, the tax and the total you pay agree in every currency, rather than
disagreeing by a unit because each was rounded on its own.

## A booking keeps the currency it was made in

The currency you were quoted in is stored with the booking, together with the
rate used at the time. Switch the site to something else afterwards and your
existing bookings still read in their original currency, at their original
rate.

That is deliberate. What you agreed to pay does not change because a rate
moved a week later, and a receipt that quietly re-prices itself is not a
receipt.

## Currencies without small change

Some currencies have no minor unit — yen, won and Icelandic króna among them.
Prices in those show no decimals at all, rather than a hopeful `.00`, and the
symbol sits where that currency puts it with the separators it uses.
