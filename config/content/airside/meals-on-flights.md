## Whether there is food at all

On short flights within a region, assume there is not — or that there is a
trolley selling sandwiches. On long-haul, assume there is, because the flight
is long enough that not feeding people becomes a welfare question rather than a
commercial one.

Between those two the answer is the fare. Airlines that unbundled everything
sell food on routes where a legacy carrier still includes it, and the same
airline can do both depending on which brand the ticket was sold under.

{.callout}
> **The reliable move on any flight under four hours is to eat before you
> board.** Everything below is about the flights where that is not practical.

## Special meals are a separate booking

A vegetarian, kosher, halal, gluten-free or low-sodium meal is requested
against the booking, usually up to about 24 hours before departure, and it is
not part of choosing a seat or checking in. Miss the window and the galley
carries what it was loaded with.

They also arrive first. Special meals are served before the main service because
they are counted and tracked individually, which is a small and genuine
advantage if you are in a hurry to sleep.

The failure mode worth knowing: a special meal ordered on the outbound does not
always survive a schedule change or a re-issued ticket onto a different flight.
It is worth re-checking after any change to an itinerary.

## Why airline food tastes the way it does

Cabin air is dry and the cabin is pressurised to something like 2,000 metres of
altitude. Both dull the perception of salt and sweetness — by a substantial
margin in most measurements — while leaving sour, bitter and umami closer to
normal.

That is why the food is seasoned harder than it would be on the ground, why
tomato juice is drunk on aircraft by people who never order it anywhere else,
and why the wine that tasted fine at altitude is disappointing at home.

## Timing, and the thing nobody tells you

Service starts after the seatbelt sign goes off and takes a while to reach the
back. On an overnight flight that is the difference between sleeping and eating,
and the choice is worth making before you board rather than when the trolley
arrives.

If sleep is the priority, tell the crew on boarding and most will leave you a
tray or skip you entirely rather than wake you[^1].

[^1]: Some airlines offer this formally as an express or sleep service; on the
rest it is a request to the crew, and it depends on how the galley is running.
